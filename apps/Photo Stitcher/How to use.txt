PHOTO STITCHER - how to use it
==============================

WHAT IT DOES
  Joins overlapping photos into one big picture, a panorama. The photos can
  go side by side, top to bottom, or in rows and columns, and you can add
  them in any order: the program works out where each one goes. It evens out
  the brightness between photos and hides the seams.
  Your photos themselves are never changed, moved or deleted.

START IT
  Double-click Run_PhotoStitcher.bat (or a shortcut to it).

JOIN PHOTOS
  1. Pick the photos: click "Add photos", or "Add a folder" to take every
     photo in a folder. To take one off the list, click it and click
     "Remove picked" (hold Ctrl to pick several).
  2. Say what kind of photos they are:
       - Taken by turning the camera: landscapes, rooms, buildings.
       - Flat things shot or scanned in parts: documents, maps, walls.
     If they fit the other kind better, the program uses that one and says so.
     Photos of different sizes (another camera, or zoomed) are fine.
  3. Click "Stitch the photos". A clock shows how long it has been working.
     A few photos take seconds; many big photos can take a few minutes.
     "Cancel" stops it at once.
  4. Look at the picture. "Trim the ragged black edges" cuts it to a clean
     rectangle (untick it to see everything). Under "Save as", pick JPG
     (smaller file), PNG (full quality, bigger file) or TIFF. Then click
     "Save the panorama". "Open it" shows the saved picture; "Open the folder"
     shows where it is. Panoramas are saved in your Pictures folder, in
     "Panoramas", unless you pick another place.

  The words under the picture say how many photos were joined, and name any
  photo that was left out and why.

STEP 5 - ADJUST (OPTIONAL)
  Click "Turn, square up, crop or resize". If the panorama isn't saved yet,
  the program saves it first, so you keep the plain panorama as well. A big
  Adjust window opens:
    1. Turn: "Turn left" or "Turn right" turns it a quarter turn. The
       Straighten slider tilts it up to 15 degrees either way; the black
       corners that leaves are trimmed off by themselves.
    2. Square up: click "Square up", drag the 4 white dots onto the corners of
       something that should be a rectangle (a building, a document), then
       click Apply. The picture is straightened so that shape is square.
    3. Crop: click "Draw a crop", drag a box over the part to keep, then click
       Apply.
    4. Size: Full size, 3/4, 1/2 or 1/4 (smaller files for email or the web).
    5. "Undo" takes back the last change; "Start over" goes back to the plain
       panorama. "Save as" saves the adjusted panorama as a new file (it
       suggests the plain one's name plus "adjusted"). "Exit" closes the window
       and asks first if the adjustments aren't saved.
  Your plain panorama is never changed.

FOR GOOD RESULTS
  - Let each photo overlap the next by about a third.
  - For landscapes, stand in one spot and turn; don't walk between photos.
  - Photos with clear details join best. Plain sky, water or a white wall
    have little to match.
  - Phone photos (JPG or iPhone HEIC), scans (PNG, TIFF, BMP) and WebP all
    work.

WHAT IT NEEDS
  1. Windows 10 or 11.
  2. Python 3.10 or newer (tested with 3.14). If this PC doesn't have it,
     Run_PhotoStitcher.bat offers to install it for you; or get it yourself from
     https://www.python.org/downloads/windows/
  3. These free add-ons. On first start Photo Stitcher offers to download and
     install them (about 72 MB, once; needs an internet connection):
       OpenCV ....... https://pypi.org/project/opencv-python-headless/
       numpy ........ https://pypi.org/project/numpy/
       Pillow ....... https://pypi.org/project/pillow/
       pillow-heif .. https://pypi.org/project/pillow-heif/ (iPhone HEIC photos)
  The Setup version (Photo Stitcher Setup.exe) has everything inside it and
  needs nothing else.

TO UPDATE IT
  Unzip the new zip into the same place as this one and choose "Replace the
  files in the destination". Your settings are kept: they are stored
  elsewhere, not in this folder.
