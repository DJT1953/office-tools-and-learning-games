FIND AND REPLACE - how to use it
================================

WHAT IT DOES
  It changes the text in many .rtf, .doc, .docx and .txt documents at once.
  It can do four things, on their own or together:

  1. WORDS, PHRASES OR MARKS - like Find and Replace in Word. Every "Acme Ltd"
     becomes "Bright Star Group", for example. It also works on marks you
     can't normally see: every line break (^l) can become a paragraph mark (^p).
     And it can find and change how text looks: font, size, bold, italic,
     underline, color, highlight and style.
  2. THE BEGINNING - everything from the start of the document up to some
     text you type is taken out, and replaced with new text (or nothing).
     For example: everything before "Dear colleagues," goes.
  3. SECTIONS BETWEEN TWO PHRASES - what is between two phrases you type is
     deleted, everywhere it appears. For example: everything between "Note:"
     and "End of note" goes.
  4. THE ENDING - everything from some text you type to the end of the
     document is taken out, and replaced with new text (or nothing).
     For example: everything after "Kind regards," goes.

  You always see every change first, and nothing is changed until you say so.

START IT
  Double-click "Find and Replace" on your Desktop. (The same shortcut is
  also in this folder.) You can also drop documents or a folder onto the
  shortcut: they go straight into the list.

1. ADD THE DOCUMENTS
   - "Add documents..." picks one or more documents.
   - "Add a folder..." adds every .rtf, .doc, .docx and .txt document in a
     folder (not the ones in folders inside it). Its window shows the files
     in each folder: open the folder, then click "Use this folder".
     (Clicking one document there and then Open adds just that document.)
   - Or drag documents or folders into the list.
   "Remove from the list" and "Clear the list" take them out of the list
   (the files themselves are not touched).

2. CHOOSE WHAT TO REPLACE
   Tick one or more of the four.

   WORDS, PHRASES OR MARKS
     Find:          what to look for
     Replace with:  what to put instead (leave it empty to delete it)
     Match case:    "Cat" only finds "Cat", not "cat"
     Whole words only:  "cat" does not find the "cat" in "concatenate"
     It works like Word: it also looks in headers, footers, footnotes and
     text boxes, and a replacement typed in small letters follows the
     capitals of what it replaces ("cat" -> "dog" turns "Cat" into "Dog").

   THE BEGINNING OF EACH DOCUMENT
     Everything before:  the text where the beginning stops, for example
                         "Dear colleagues,". Copy it from a document if you
                         like. Everything before it is replaced.
     and this text too:  tick it to replace that text as well. (Not ticked:
                         the document starts with that text afterwards.)
     If the text is there more than once: use the first one (the usual
                         choice for a beginning) or the last one.

   SECTIONS BETWEEN TWO PHRASES
     First phrase, Last phrase:  what is between them is deleted, every time
                         they appear in a document.
     and the first phrase too, and the last phrase too:  tick them to delete
                         that phrase as well. (Not ticked: the phrase stays.)
     Match case:         "Note:" does not find "NOTE:". Without it, capitals
                         don't count, so "to" is also found inside "into".
                         Choose phrases that can't turn up in other words, and
                         check what the preview shows in red.

     How sections are paired: each first phrase goes with the next last phrase
     after it, and the search carries on after that one. A first phrase with no
     last phrase after it stays, and the preview says so.

     The text on each side joins up as you would leave it: no doubled spaces,
     and no empty line where a whole paragraph went. A section that starts in
     one paragraph and ends partway through a later one joins them into one
     paragraph, which takes the look of the later one.

     Good to know about sections:
       - Only the main text is looked at, not headers, footers or footnotes.
       - A whole table inside a section goes with it. A section that starts or
         ends across the edge of a table, or runs from one cell to another,
         stays as it is, and the preview says so.
       - The special codes below work here too, such as ^m for a page break.

   THE ENDING OF EACH DOCUMENT
     Everything after:   the text where the ending starts, for example
                         "Kind regards," or "Unsubscribe". Everything after
                         it is replaced.
     and this text too:  tick it to replace that text as well.
     If the text is there more than once: use the last one (the usual
                         choice for an ending) or the first one.

   REPLACE IT WITH (for the beginning and the ending)
     - this text: type or paste the new beginning or ending. It can be
       several lines long. Leave it empty to just delete the beginning or
       ending. Typed text gets the look of the ordinary text next to it
       (not of a heading).
     - the contents of a document: click "Choose..." and pick a Word
       document. Everything in it is put in, with its own fonts, styles
       and layout. Handy for a standard introduction or ending.

   Straight and curly quotes count as the same ("it's" finds "it's").
   The text to look for can be up to 255 characters long.
   The app remembers everything you typed for next time.

   SPECIAL MARKS AND CODES (the same codes as in Word)
     All the boxes understand Word's special codes. Type them, or click the
     "Special" button next to a box and pick one from the list. For example:
       Turn line breaks into paragraph marks:   Find ^l     Replace with ^p
       Take out empty lines:                    Find ^p^p   Replace with ^p
       Turn tabs into spaces:                   Find ^t     Replace with a space
     Like Word, ^p^p replaces each pair once. If there are several empty lines
     in a row, preview and run it again on the changed documents.

       ^p  paragraph mark (Enter)            ^s  non-breaking space
       ^l  manual line break (Shift+Enter)   ^~  non-breaking hyphen
       ^t  tab                               ^-  optional hyphen
       ^m  manual page break                 ^+  em dash
       ^n  column break                      ^=  en dash
       ^^  the ^ character itself

     Only in the boxes that look for something:
       ^b  section break     ^w  white space (spaces and tabs together)
       ^?  any character     ^#  any digit       ^$  any letter
       ^g  picture           ^f  footnote mark   ^e  endnote mark
     Only in "Replace with" for words, phrases or marks:
       ^&  the text that was found ("cat" replaced with "^&s" makes "cats")
     Numbers work as in Word too, such as ^13, ^0150 and ^u8212.

     To use the ^ character itself, type ^^. A code that doesn't exist is
     pointed out before anything happens. Word's clipboard code ^c is not
     available: paste the text into the box instead.

   FORMATTING (the "Format..." buttons, like in Word)
     "Find" and "Replace with" each have a "Format..." button. You can choose
     a font, size, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, color, highlight
     and style. Anything left at "(any)" or "(no change)" is ignored.

     How the boxes and the formats work together:
       Find "cat" + Format Bold       finds only the bold "cat"s
       Find empty + Format Bold       finds all bold text
       Replace with empty + a Format  keeps the words and only changes their look
       Replace with "dog" + a Format  puts in "dog" with that look
       Replace with empty, no Format  deletes what was found, as always

     For example, to make all bold text red instead: leave both boxes empty,
     choose Bold under "Find", and choose Not bold and Red under "Replace with".

     The chosen format shows in blue under the box, and is remembered for next
     time. To take it away, click "Format..." and then "No formatting".

     Good to know about formats:
       - "Automatic color" is the usual text color. To Word it is not the same
         as "Black", so ordinary text is found with "Automatic color".
       - Highlights are found as Highlighted or Not highlighted, not by color.
       - "Underlined" finds a single underline, not double or dotted ones.
       - Giving text a paragraph style, such as a heading, changes the whole
         paragraph it is in, even if only one word was found.
       - A style someone made themselves may only be in some documents. Those
         without it are left alone, and the preview says so.
       - The "Format..." buttons are for words, phrases and marks. To give a
         new beginning or ending its own look, use "the contents of a document".
       - A .txt file has no formatting. Nothing in it is found by a format, and
         new text goes in without one.

3. PREVIEW THE CHANGES
   Click "Preview the changes...". Each document is read (nothing is changed
   yet), and a list shows what will change in each one. Click a document to
   see the details:
     red and crossed out  = taken out
     green                = put in
     blue                 = gets the format shown in [ ]
     grey                 = stays as it is
   Marks show by name, such as [line break] and [paragraph].
   A long beginning or ending shows its first and last lines, with the
   number of characters in between. If the text was not found in a
   document, it says so, and that part of the document stays as it is.

4. CHOOSE WHERE TO SAVE, AND MAKE THE CHANGES
   - Save changed copies in a folder called "replaced" (the choice that is
     ticked when the window opens). Your documents stay exactly as they
     are. The changed copies go into a "replaced" folder next to them, with
     the same names. A copy left there by an earlier run is replaced.
   - Change the documents themselves. You are asked to confirm first.
     Before each document is changed, a copy of it as it is now is kept in
     "before replace\<date and time>" next to it.
   Then click "Save the changed copies" or "Change the documents".
   Only documents that have something to change are saved.

5. READ THE SUMMARY
   It lists what was changed in each document, and anything that could
   not be done, and why. "Open the folder" shows the changed documents.
   After changing the documents themselves, "Put the documents back as
   they were" undoes it. You can also do that yourself later: copy the
   documents from the "before replace" folder back over the changed ones.

GOOD TO KNOW
  - Microsoft Word makes the changes to .rtf, .doc and .docx files in the
    background (you won't see it). A document keeps its type: a .doc is
    saved as a .doc, an .rtf as an .rtf. .txt files are changed directly,
    and keep their special letters and line breaks.
  - Close a document in Word before changing it with "Change the documents
    themselves" (a document that is open can't be changed).
  - Password-protected documents can't be opened, so they are skipped.
  - A beginning or ending can't be cut in the middle of a table. If the
    text is inside a table, that document is left as it is (the preview
    says so).
  - With tracked changes, the replacing is not tracked: it is simply done.
  - Some marks always stay, because replacing them would damage the document:
    the last paragraph mark of a document, header, footer or text box; the
    paragraph mark right before a table; the mark that ends a footnote or
    endnote before the next one; and the ends of table cells.
  - In a .txt file every line ending counts as ^p. A .txt file has no separate
    line breaks, so ^l finds nothing there, and ^l in "Replace with" makes an
    ordinary new line.
  - The beginning, the ending and the sections are cut first, then words and
    phrases are replaced in what is left. A new beginning or ending you put in is not
    changed by the word replacements.

IF THE DESKTOP SHORTCUT GOES MISSING (or you move this folder)
  Double-click "Create desktop shortcut" in this folder.

TRY IT SAFELY
  The "Try it - sample documents" folder has three newsletters (.docx, .doc
  and .rtf) and some meeting notes (.txt). Each newsletter starts with a
  title and "Please do not forward..." before "Dear colleagues,", and ends
  with "You are receiving this newsletter..." and an Unsubscribe line.
  Try this:
    - Words, phrases or marks: find "Acme Holdings", replace with "Bright Star Group"
    - The beginning: everything before "Dear colleagues,", replaced with the
      contents of the document "For the new beginning\Bright Star beginning.docx"
    - The ending: everything after "You are receiving this", and this text
      too, replaced with nothing
  Then look at the copies in the "replaced" folder.

  "Copied from the web.rtf" has line breaks instead of paragraph marks.
  Try finding ^l and replacing it with ^p, with only "Words, phrases or
  marks" ticked.

  "Formatted text.rtf" has headings, bold words and a red warning. Try leaving
  both boxes empty, choosing Bold with the "Format..." button next to "Find",
  and choosing Not bold and Blue with the one next to "Replace with".

  In the newsletters, try first phrase "New starters" and last phrase "Spring
  picnic", with "and the first phrase too" ticked. In the preview, see how it
  also finds "new starters" in the line near the top. Ticking "Match case"
  stops that.
